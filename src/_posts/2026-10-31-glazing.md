## Glazing

Glazing is an important technique in painting. It allows you to add a transparent layer of paint over your existing layers. Often, you will use it to enhance the color below it for things like shadows, or warm up your colors with a yellow or orange beneath. 

Before you apply a glaze, you want to wait for your existing layers to be dry to the touch. You normally wait to apply a glaze on the last layer of your painting. 

To make a glaze, you use transparent or semi-transparent paints. You can either find this information on the tube or look it up on the manufacturer's website. A good brand will have this information for you. You should avoid using opaque paint like titanium white or cadmiums in a glaze. 

When mixing up your paint, you want to add a medium that will make the paint a transparent film. I dip my brush into my medium, which is usually Gamblin Galkyd, and pull out a thin layer from the mixture. You need quite a bit of medium for this. Then you apply it to the area you want to glaze with.

I often glaze with a mixture of ultramarine blue and burnt umber for shadows. I also use a transparent red or a mixture of sap green and ultramarine blue.

There is a painting method called a grisaille. It's a monochrome painting like using burnt umber and titanium white. The goal is to make a gray or neutral colored painting to get the values correct. After that, you apply glazes of color over the monochrome painting.

I think glazing makes your colors really pop.
