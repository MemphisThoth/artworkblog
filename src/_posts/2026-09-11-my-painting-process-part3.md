# My Painting Process, Part 3

This is the third post about my painting process. The [first part](https://jaredsartblog.net/2026/08/22/my-painting-process-part1) and the [second part](https://jaredsartblog.net/2026/08/24/my-painting-process-p2) are linked here.

While waiting for the first layer to dry, I bought a Princeton Catalyst #16 flat brush. Here is a picture of it next to a Catalyst #4 flat brush. It's 37mm wide. This brush let me cover the canvas quickly in the large areas. I love that it is firm with quite a bit of spring to it.

![Princeton Catalyst 16 flat brush](/assets/images/painting-process-p3/catalyst16brush.jpg)

I started working on the second layer of my painting. My goal for the second layer is to get the colors closer to my reference photograph.

I added another coat on the sky. I used ultramarine blue and titanium white at the top, and as I worked my way down, I mixed in some cerulean blue and titanium white.

I put another layer of paint on the water too. This time I used mostly prussian blue and a bit of titanium white. 

![step four](/assets/images/painting-process-p3/step-four.jpg)

I learned that if you hold the canvas up to the light, you can see which areas are still thin with paint. I did this, and I found the bottom part of the sky could use more paint.

However, I wanted to cover the other elements of the painting. I thought her coat and shirt were too dark, and the boat needed more paint. 

So, I mixed up two-parts ultramarine blue to one-part of burnt umber to get a black color. I used up the rest of my Grumbacher titanium white. I mixed a touch of the black into the white, and covered the boat with a very light gray color.

I thought her coat was too dark. I tried mixing ultramarine blue and cadmium yellow light to make a green color, but it seemed more muted than I wanted. So, I wiped off the new layer of green.

Then, I mixed up some ultramarine blue and yellow ochre. This made a green for her coat I was looking for. I added quite a bit of my new [Williamsburg titanium white](https://goldenartistcolors.com/products/williamsburg-artist-oil-colors-titanium-white) to the green. 

I painted her coat. I used some darker green for the shadows of her coat like the pockets, and I blended the shadows on the inside of her coat below her left arm.

![step five](/assets/images/painting-process-p3/step-five.jpg)

Her shirt had another layer of orange painted on it. I used indian yellow and titanium white. 

I am still not happy with her shirt. Now, I am thinking about changing it to a blue color. This would make a more analogous color scheme.

I am figuring this stuff out as I go. I am jokingly wondering if I will have ten layers of paint before I think the painting is done.
